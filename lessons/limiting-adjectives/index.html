Limiting Adjectives 🧭

These words identify, count, or point out a noun. They usually appear where an article would go.

A word that points to the noun

A limiting adjective (also called a determiner in modern grammar) comes before a noun and narrows down which one, whose, how many, or what kind of reference is meant. For example, cette maison means “this house.”

Demonstrative

ce livre, this book

Exclamative

Quel beau jour !, What a lovely day!

Indefinite

plusieurs amis, several friends

Interrogative

Quelle heure ?, What time?

Numerical

trois pommes, three apples

Possessive

mon frère, my brother

Relative (traditional grammar)

Lequel contrat ?, Which contract? The relative use of lequel before a noun is rare and formal today. Here it links back to a previously mentioned noun.

The categories can look alike: quel is interrogative in a question and exclamative when expressing a strong reaction. These words agree with the noun where their form changes.
