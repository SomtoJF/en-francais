French Adverbs 🗺️

Adverbs tell when, where, how, how often, or to what extent something happens.

One part of speech, five useful jobs

An adverb adds information about an action or description. For example, Elle parle lentement means “She speaks slowly.” Lentement tells how she speaks.

Frequency: how often?

souvent (often), toujours (always), parfois (sometimes), jamais (never).
Je lis souvent. I often read.

Manner: how?

bien (well), vite (quickly), lentement (slowly).
Il conduit prudemment. He drives carefully.

Place: where?

ici (here), dehors (outside), partout (everywhere), nulle part (nowhere).
Elle travaille ici. She works here.

Quantity or degree: how much?

beaucoup (a lot), peu (little), très (very), trop (too).
Il travaille beaucoup. He works a lot.

Time: when?

aujourd’hui (today), demain (tomorrow), déjà (already), bientôt (soon).
Nous partons demain. We leave tomorrow.

Where adverbs go

Placement depends on what the adverb modifies and whether the verb is simple or compound.

Simple tenses

Many short adverbs follow the conjugated verb:

Elle chante bien (She sings well)

Je lis souvent (I often read)

Compound tenses

Many short adverbs fit between the auxiliary and past participle:

J’ai souvent voyagé (I have often travelled)

Elle a déjà fini (She has already finished)

Longer adverbs often follow the participle.

Quantity

Use beaucoup de before a noun:

beaucoup de livres

Use beaucoup after a verb:

Il lit beaucoup

Très modifies an adjective or adverb: très intéressant , très vite .

Time and place

These often appear at the beginning or end:

Demain, nous partons (Tomorrow, we leave)

Elle travaille ici (She works here)

Special pattern: ne … personne

Personne means “nobody” or “no one.” When it is the object, put it after the verb: Je ne vois personne. = “I see no one.” With a compound tense, put it after the past participle: Je n’ai vu personne. = “I saw no one.”

When “nobody” is the subject, it comes first and ne goes before the verb: Personne ne vient. = “Nobody is coming.” Use ne … jamais for “never” and ne … nulle part for “nowhere.”
