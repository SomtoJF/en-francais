L'Accent Grave ↘ è à ù

The falling accent: it changes the sound on e, but on a and u it's a silent guardian of meaning.

Two Jobs, One Accent

On e — it changes the sound

è produces the open [ɛ] "air" sound: mère, père, très, près. Compare with é ("ay") — the direction of the tilt tells you the sound.

On a and u — it changes the meaning

à and ù sound exactly the same as unaccented a and u. The accent exists only to distinguish words with identical spellings but different meanings: a (has) vs à (to), ou (or) vs où (where), la (the) vs là (there).

Same Sound, Different Word

a/à, ou/où, la/là — the sound won't help you here. Pick the spelling whose meaning fits the sentence.

è or é? Which Way Does the Hat Tilt?

The classic mix-up: grave (è ↘, "air") vs aigu (é ↗, "ay"). Listen to the word, feel the sound, and pick the right accent.
